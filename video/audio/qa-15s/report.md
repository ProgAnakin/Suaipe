## Warnings / caveats

- No automatic check failed.
- Not verifiable here: how it *sounds* (taste, harshness, fatigue). Everything was judged from measurements and spectrograms; a listen on a phone speaker, headphones and monitors is still recommended before publishing.
- The ffmpeg used for metering is an old static build (2018); its ebur128 agrees with an independent BS.1770 implementation (own meter row above) to within 0.05 LU.
- Music vs SFX balance: music stem active RMS -22.5 dBFS (stem scale, sum peaking at -1 dBFS); the loudest SFX peak is -3.5 dBFS (19 dB above the music RMS) and the typical UI transient (tap, tick, key, pop ...) peaks at -15.2 dBFS (7 dB above the music RMS). The brief's 'music roughly 18-20 dB below the SFX peaks' holds against the big hits; literally 18-20 dB under every UI sound would make -14 LUFS unreachable, so UI sounds are kept clear by spectral placement and baked ducking instead.

## Sync check (sound-design accent vs cue time, measured on stems/sfx.wav)

`err` = detected onset minus the cue's target time (for swipes the target is the `accent` field, for risers the moment the energy peaks). The detector looks at three bands (45-300 Hz, 0.3-1.8 kHz, 1.8-12 kHz), takes the positive novelty of each envelope and reports the first moment the cleanest burst reaches 30 % of its peak.

**Max |error| over the 7 representative cues above: 2.13 ms (mean 1.05 ms)** - requirement < 10 ms.

The same measurement on **every** transient cue of the sheet (`sync-measured.csv`): 12 of 14 could be separated in the mix and measured - max |err| 3.75 ms, mean 1.36 ms, 95th percentile 3.29 ms; the others sit under a louder simultaneous sound (the isolated check below covers them).
