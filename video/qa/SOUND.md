## 1. What you chose, in one paragraph

**A · Minimal pulse.** No drums: a soft sub pulse, a warm slowly breathing pad, felt electric piano playing sparse chord tones, a glass bell for the motif and the
room tone of a shop under the photographs. The eight swipes *are* the rhythm (tuned yes / no phrases). The film has one big moment, at the end — the handshake
chord, then the logo — and a smaller one at the 98 %. "Minimal" describes the **music** and the **hierarchy**, not the number of effects: under it the interface has a sound for everything
you can see, in one family (glass, felt, air), in the key of the bar, each at the loudness of its class (SOUND-AUDIT.md, section 4).

**Three words of atmosphere** (for the track *and* for the post): **Observant · Composed · Warm.** Observant: the sound listens before it speaks (hook: drone, one soft pulse,
air). Composed: few elements, each with room around it; nothing that sounds like a game show. Warm: the ending is people, not interface — room tone, felt keys, a dry clasp, a chord that resolves.

## 3. The sonic motif — "contact" (4 notes)

* **Notes.** G4 – A4 – C5 – E5 (sol-la-do-mi): a rising line inside the C6 sound of the film's key.
* **Rhythm.** short · short · medium · long — 0.25 s, 0.25 s, 0.5 s, then the last note rings (≥ 1.5 s).
* **Timbre.** Glass bell (the film's own bell voice, partials 1 : 2.76 : 5.4) doubled by a felt electric piano, soft attack, no vibrato; velocity grows slightly to the last note.
* **Where it appears.**
  * *Lock-up (4.5):* **part 1 only** — G4 → A4, over the C of the logo hit: a question left hanging.
  * *End card (61.0):* the **complete** line on the logo, G4 at 61.0 → E5 held on Cadd9 — the circle closes.
  * *15 s cut:* the same complete line at 11.5 s (the logo), the chord rings to the end.
* **Reuse.** Same notes, same bell, same rhythm can open and close future films and posts; vary only the octave.

## 4. Effects: every event, one hierarchy

The sound design has **no budget of effects, only a hierarchy of loudness**: every event of the picture gets a sound (`audio/picture-events.json` lists the 250 events, `qa/SOUND-MAP.md` the sound of each),
and what keeps the whole calm is that every sound is **levelled to its class** and sits in one family.

| class | rule | sounds in this film | level (A+K-weighted, dBFS before the bus) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S · signature** (max 6) | the only ones with sub, long tail and chord; levelled by hand with the music | the chosen product (2.0) · the logo hit (4.0) and the motif's question (4.5) · the 98 % (22.0) · the clasp (60.0) · the motif on the logo (61.0) | by hand |
| **H · highlight** | an arrival or a confirmation: bright, clearly the brightest thing around it | success chime, notification, discount code, "redeemed in store" | −17 |
| **A · action** | something the viewer does or reads: a tap, a language, a tick in a box, a swipe, a label or a chip popping in, a node or a tile lighting, a lead landing | `tap`, `chip-tick`, `check-tick`, `lock-click`, `callout-in`, `chip-pop`, `swipe-yes/no`, `node-on`, `tile-on`, `crm-land`, `word-hit`, `screen-wake`, `device-settle` | −20.5 |
| **W · movement** | air that follows the picture: a page, a camera move, a scroll | `whoosh-*`, `zoom-whoosh`, `page-swoosh`, `scroll-soft`, `lead-fly`, `photo-whoosh`, `swoosh-open` | −19.5 |
| **T · texture** | small, many, heard and never the point: keystrokes, the scan counter, the gadget cards, the letters of the name, progress dots, captions, packets, glints | `key` (33), `count-tick` (13), `tile-pop` (10), `letter-tick` (12), `dot-tick` (8), `caption-pop` (12), `card-in` (8), `packet`, `shimmer`, `tagline-air` | −25 |
| **t · whisper** | the quietest touches | `field-tick` (a field waking up), `line-draw` (a line drawing itself), `sample-tick` (the "Sample data" chip), `underline` (the pen under a caption) | −27.5 |

**The music makes room by class** (`mix.sfx_ducks_a`): a run of keystrokes takes 3.5 dB of the pad and the felt piano; an action dips the felt piano 3 dB for 0.2 s and breathes the pad 1.5 dB; a movement
takes 2 dB of the pad while it sweeps (3.5 dB for a whoosh that carries a scene change); a caption's underline takes 5 dB of the felt piano; the highlights, the hits and the motif keep their deeper ducks.

**Swipe yes / no, readable with eyes closed.** *Yes* = a short **rising** two-note glass phrase that opens (tuned to the bar's chord), panned **right**, a little air on the tail.
*No* = a short **falling** muted pluck with a soft closing noise, panned **left**. Different contour, different timbre, different side; mono compatibility stays ≥ −3 dB.

**Phone-speaker rule.** Every signature sound carries upper harmonics (no cue loses more than 7 dB through a phone-speaker filter, HP 350 Hz + LP 10 kHz; the worst used to lose 9.4 dB).

## 5. Human scenes: room, foley, hand-over from interface to world

* **Room tone.** A synthesised shop: large-room air, a faint diffuse murmur (filtered, modulated noise — no intelligible words), a distant soft reflection, felt rather than heard.
  It enters with the hand-off photograph (5.4) and returns under the bag and the handshake (57.5) — the start and the end of the film rhyme.
* **Foley.** `bag-rustle` (58.0) and the clasp (60.0) stay close, dry and believable; nothing bright or "magic" on the handshake itself — the warmth comes from the music.
* **Interface → world.** When the camera flies into the tablet (6.85) the room tone gives way to the screen's own sounds; on the way out (photo-whoosh 59.4) the reverse.

### Listening guide (one page)

**Phone speaker** (the way most people will hear it)
1. The language selection (8.8 – 9.6 s): five distinct ticks sweeping left to right?
2. Can you tell a *yes* swipe from a *no* swipe with your eyes closed (15.5 – 20 s) — and did the tutorial (13.6 – 14.0 s) already teach you which is which?
3. Is the 98 % (22.0) clearly the first peak — and the handshake (60.0) clearly bigger?
4. Do the notification (26.9 – 27.0) and the code ding (32.2) sound like "something arrived" rather than "an app"?
5. Is anything harsh or shrill when the volume is high?

**Earbuds (AirPods or similar)**
6. The typing (10.1 – 11.9 s): natural, or too much?
7. Do you feel the shop under the photographs (hand-off 5.4 – 8; bag and handshake 57.5 – 61) without being able to point at it?
8. Do the first and last seconds rhyme (the question G–A at 4.5 → the complete motif at 61.0)?
9. Do the Manager / Stats (34 – 44) and Consultants (44 – 54) scenes feel alive and competent, or too sparse / too busy?

**Laptop speakers**
10. Does anything vanish (sub) or poke out (high glass)?
11. Is any single sound irritating the **second** time you watch? (The most disposable families, if the whole is busy: the caption underline, the "whispers", the letter ticks.)
12. Does the ending give a sense of conclusion, and does the silence after it feel clean?

Tell me, per question: *fine · change it · remove it*. That is all I need for the final render.

## 8. Regenerate

```bash
npm run events                                  # src/timeline.ts → audio/picture-events.json (250 events)
npm run cues                                    # → audio/cues.json (246 cues) and audio/cues-15s.json (89), with audio/trims.json applied
python tools/audio/generate.py --ffmpeg $FFMPEG # direction A: master + stems + audio/qa (about 90 s)
python tools/audio/generate.py --cues audio/cues-15s.json --name soundtrack-15s --qa-dir audio/qa-15s --build-dir audio/build15 --ffmpeg $FFMPEG
python tools/audio/generate.py --from-stems --sfx-gain-db -3 --remix-name soundtrack_effects-3.wav   # an alternative balance, no synthesis (also +2)
python tools/audio/catalogue.py --ffmpeg $FFMPEG                       # qa/SFX-CATALOGUE.md and out/audio-ab/sfx-catalogue.wav
python scripts/qa/av-coverage.py --md --json out/av.json                # every picture event: silent / masked / loud; then scripts/qa/auto-trim.py and npm run cues
python scripts/qa/sound-map.py                                         # qa/SOUND-MAP.md
```

Then `scripts/render-preview.sh` (silent half-resolution pictures) and `scripts/audio-previews.sh` (the files of section 6).
